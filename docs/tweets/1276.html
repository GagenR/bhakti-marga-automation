Navaratri comes four times a year with 2 of them often being Gupt (hidden).
Chaitra Navaratri (around March-April) is celebrated close to the new year of the Sanatan Dharma. It includes the celebration of Sri Ram Navami, the appearance of Bhagavan Sri Ram in this world.
Sharad Navratri (around September-October) is the most widely celebrated Navratri, concluding with Dussehra marking the victory of Sri Ram over the terrible asura Ravan.
The other two Navaratri are celebrated around January-February and June-July.
The four together mark the various seasons and blessings bestowed by Bhagavan at those times. Some are celebrated intensely, while others remain mostly hidden. Sometimes the Divine Mother enters one’s life subtly, not perceived by the mind. This is often the way the SatGuru takes. Hidden from the mind and under the radar of conscious perception. This His Gupt, secret, grace. Don’t think that you can understand the Master. It’s not possible. Just as it is impossible to understand the Divine Mother and how She leads all Her children in such different ways. Yet to the same destination, Bhagavan’s Lotus Feet. People judge things based on the outside. But the SatGuru is unconcerned by this. His eyes are forever fixed on the eternal. He is the grace that flows from Bhagavan. He is the mercy that leads one back to Sri Hari. He is within the circle of life and beyond it. He is the triangle of personal Deity, Antaryami, and cosmic Parabrahman. How to understand that which cannot be understood? This realization itself is the presence of the Master as the Divine Mother.
During Navaratri, the SatGuru leads everyone into the softness of the heart, the fierceness of self-transformation, and the ultimate self-sacrifice into Love. May all offer that restless mind into the most loving arms of Maa. Shubh Navaratri.
