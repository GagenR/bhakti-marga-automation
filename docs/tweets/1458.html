Offerings and stories to remember on Ganesh Chaturthi.
A modak is a traditional sweet that is offered to Lord Ganesha. The modak symbolizes spiritual wisdom and bliss, the sweet reward of discipline. Puja rituals often include 21 modaks, symbolizing the five organs of action, five senses, five vital airs, five elements, and the mind. The word modak comes from Sanskrit: moda meaning happiness, joy, or delight, and ka is a suffix meaning ‘that which gives.’ Together: that which gives happiness. Modak and Mudra come from the same root in Sanskrit — to be joyful, to delight in, to be glad. Through Mudras (Sadhana) and Modak (Seva) we offer to Bhagavan that which makes us happy, and in turn He gives what makes Him happy. We give the small happiness of this world and He gives back His happiness, the eternal SatChidAnanda.
There are so many stories of Ganesha that all can learn from. Removing Kuber’s pride of wealth and money. Removing Chandra Dev’s pride over appearance and beauty. Defeating anger personified as Krodhasura. Transforming the doubt and sorrow of Rishi Ved Vyas into bliss by becoming his scribe to write the Mahabharat. Controlling restlessness in the form of the mouse as his Vahan as well as mastering laziness by taking the form of a crow and waking up Rishi Agastya from untimely sleep. Ganesha is the first Deity to be worshipped in any Puja because He is wisdom itself. But, Ganesha himself puts the SatGuru before him, the only One who is greater than him. The SatGuru is the true auspiciousness, the eternal Vighnaharta, in the life of a bhakta. With eyes of love and a heart filled with sweetness, one can worship His eternal form within the Self at all times. This continuous Manas Puja brings every blessing, because the inner Guru receives and accepts everything given with love.
