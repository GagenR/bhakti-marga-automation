UNDER THE MOUNTAIN – THE SHELTER OF DIVINE GRACE
Today we remember one of the sweetest and most profound leelas of Bhagwan Sri Krishna, the Govardhan Leela. It is not merely the story of a little boy lifting a mountain; it is the revelation of what happens when the Divine becomes the refuge of those who place their complete trust in Him. When Indra discovered that the people of Vraja had not performed the traditional worship offered to him, his pride was wounded. He could not accept that their devotion had been directed towards Govardhan and Sri Krishna instead. In his anger, he unleashed terrifying storms upon Vraja, believing that his power could bring the entire land to its knees. But what Indra failed to understand was that the very power he commanded had its source in the Supreme Lord. As the heavens poured down relentlessly, the people of Vraja did not turn towards their own strength; they turned towards Krishna. And Krishna, with the simplicity and playfulness of a child, lifted Govardhan Hill on His little finger and offered shelter to everyone. The Śrīmad Bhāgavatam beautifully describes this divine act: “He lifted Govardhana with one hand and held it up playfully, as a child holds up a mushroom.” (Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 10.25.19). For seven days and seven nights, the entire community remained beneath that mountain. There was no fear, no distinction and no anxiety about what the next moment would bring. The rain continued, but their hearts had found their shelter. What a beautiful reminder that divine protection does not always mean the immediate disappearance of our difficulties. Sometimes Bhagwan allows the circumstances to unfold, but He gives us the strength, grace and shelter to pass through them. We often measure God’s love by how quickly He answers our prayers, how soon a difficulty disappears or how perfectly life follows our expectations. Yet Govardhan Leela teaches us to recognise a deeper miracle: even when everything around us seems to be falling apart, the Lord can hold an entire mountain above us with effortless grace. This is the essence of śaraṇāgati. Surrender does not mean that we understand everything happening in our lives; it means that even when understanding fails, our trust in the Divine remains unshaken. The people of Vraja did not need to know how Krishna would protect them for seven days. They only needed to remain close to Him. In our own lives, the mountain may take a different form. It may be a difficult period, an unanswered prayer, a disappointment, a separation, a responsibility or a situation beyond our control. We may not always be able to change the storm, but we can change where we stand in relation to it. When we take refuge in Sri Krishna and remain under the loving guidance of our SadGuru, we discover that grace is not always the absence of hardship; sometimes it is the unseen strength that carries us through it. The SadGuru continually reminds us to look beyond the temporary storms of the world and recognise the eternal hand that sustains us. There is also a profound lesson in the mountain itself. Govardhan nourished Vraja through its forests, grasses, water and natural abundance. Krishna revealed that true worship is not simply about rituals performed for rewards, but about recognising and honouring the divine presence in everything that sustains life. Gratitude becomes worship when we learn to see God’s grace in the ordinary gifts we so often take for granted. The cows that give nourishment, the earth that provides food, the water that sustains us and the people who walk beside us are all reminders of divine generosity. And when the mountain became a shelter, it revealed another truth: whatever has been given to us as nourishment can also become an instrument of service. Indra’s experience is equally significant. His mistake was not simply that he became angry; it was that he forgot the source of his authority.
