Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

zaterdag 21 november 2026, 11:55
Today, as we celebrate the sacred occasion of Tulsi Vivah, we rejoice not merely in the divine marriage of Tulsi Devi and Shreeman Narayan, but in the revelation of the most intimate and eternal relationship between the Atma and Paramatma. Within every living being resides a longing that no worldly experience, achievement or relationship can ever completely satisfy, for its origin lies beyond the material realm. It is the longing of the soul to return to its Beloved, to dwell eternally in His presence and to experience the immeasurable sweetness of His divine Prem. Tulsi Devi embodies the perfection of this longing, where every desire has been transformed into devotion and every aspect of existence has become an offering at the lotus feet of Bhagavan. Her eternal union with Shreeman Narayan reveals the beauty of a heart that seeks nothing from the Lord except the privilege of loving Him. Such love is beyond the boundaries of time, circumstances and worldly understanding. It neither diminishes through difficulties nor increases through personal gains, for its foundation is the eternal truth of the soul itself. Throughout countless lifetimes, the Atma wanders in search of happiness, becoming entangled in attachments, identities and desires, forgetting the very purpose for which it has received this precious human existence. Yet, through the infinite compassion of Bhagavan, the SatGuru appears in one’s life to awaken the remembrance of that forgotten relationship. The presence of the SatGuru is an invitation to turn one’s consciousness away from the temporary and towards the eternal, to discover that the greatest treasure is not something to be attained externally, but a divine relationship that has always existed. When Bhakti matures into pure Prem, devotion is no longer confined to moments of prayer, rituals or sacred occasions; it becomes the very essence of one’s living. Every thought becomes remembrance, every action becomes Seva, every difficulty becomes an opportunity for surrender, and every breath becomes an expression of gratitude. The heart no longer asks Bhagavan to fulfil its personal wishes but instead prays to become an instrument of His divine will. This is the profound beauty of Sharanagati to surrender not only one’s suffering and worries, but also one’s ambitions, expectations and sense of ownership, allowing Bhagavan to occupy the very centre of one’s existence. The sacred marriage of Tulsi Devi teaches us that spiritual fulfilment is found not in possessing the Beloved, but in belonging entirely to Him. Just as Tulsi leaves adorn the lotus feet of Shreeman Narayan, may our lives become an offering worthy of His acceptance. May our devotion be as pure as the fragrance of Tulsi, our faith as unwavering as Her dedication, and our remembrance of Bhagavan as continuous as Her eternal service. The celebration of Tulsi Vivah also invites us to look within and contemplate the direction of our spiritual journey. Have we truly understood the purpose of receiving this human birth? Have we allowed divine love to transform our consciousness, our relationships and the way we perceive the world? Do we seek Bhagavan for the blessings He bestows, or have we reached the understanding that His presence itself is the greatest blessing? The true wealth of a bhakta lies in the recognition that one belongs to Bhagavan, irrespective of the changing circumstances of life. Even amidst the uncertainties of this world, the heart that has taken refuge at the lotus feet of the SatGuru discovers a sanctuary that no external disturbance can destroy. Spiritual awakening is the gradual blossoming of this recognition, until the remembrance of the Beloved becomes more precious than all the pleasures of creation.
