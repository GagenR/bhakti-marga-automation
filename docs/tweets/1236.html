The Chaar Prahar of Maha Shivratri.
The first meaning of the four watches of the night is that they lead the atma through the four phases of life: Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanprastha, and Sanyas. These are reflected by the four aspects shown by Lord Shiva: Bholenath/Pashupatinath, Natraj/Sundareshwar, Dakshinamurty, Kaal Bhairav. Each has its own importance. Its own blessing. The second meaning is seen through the Leelas of Vraj of Sri Krishna and Lord Shiva. Both meanings intertwine in a very beautiful way.
The first Prahar of Maha Shivratri is from 6pm-9pm. As Bholenath, Lord Shiva is the most innocent one. The one who loves all, accepts all. Pashupatinath is the father to all innocent animals and trees. The one who cares for everyone, even the smallest rocks, as a part of Sri Hari’s cosmic body. The one who sees his own Tattva within all. Brahmacharya as a young child or student is the phase of learning and growth. Where the mind is shaped by wisdom, discipline, kindness, and persistence. In the Hari Bhakta Sampradaya, Akhanda (endless) Brahmacharya is taken as a vow to signify the return to innocence, to clean away all the worldly dust, and stay permanently with God.
In the first Prahar, Gopeshwar Mahadev is worshiped. Lord Shiva was stopped by the gopis from entering the Raas Leela with Sri Krishna because there can be only One Eternal Purusha. Accepting the truth of their words, Lord Shiva bathed in the Vamshivat Ghat and became a gopi. He was allowed entry because he had fully accepted Sri Hari’s majesty as the only Purusha. Lord Shiva thus became known as Gopeshwar Mahadev (Lord of the Gopis). His temple is crucial in Vraj as it bridges the worship of Lord Shiva with devotion to Sri Krishna. It is necessary to seek Lord Shiva’s permission as the guardian of Vraj Dham to fully enter the spiritual atmosphere of Vrindavan. 
Gopeshwar Mahadev is the one who is most innocent. Fully focused on Bhagavan and His Leelas. Not wanting to be away from his Beloved Bhagavan for even a moment, Lord Shiva became the most innocent one. The one closest to Sri Krishna in the Raas. It is this humble, innocent, ever-pure love that Bhagavan longs for. The first Prahar also celebrates the awakening of Lord Shiva from Samadhi. The arrival of Bhakti and Prem when determination and persistence have given it their all.
