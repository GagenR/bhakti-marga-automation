Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

zaterdag 3 oktober 2026, 00:19
The SatGuru holds within His heart infinite seeds of spiritual wisdom and love. Yet He is a wise Divine Farmer. He does not sow His seeds where the soil is barren, neglected, or filled with weeds, nor where the field is already crowded and unable to receive anything more. He looks for the field that has been prepared by its steward , the weeds removed, the soil softened, and the dryness relieved by water. Sometimes the water is close and easy to reach; sometimes it is far away and requires great effort to bring it to the field. In the same way, devotion may sometimes feel very close to the bhakta, while at other times it may seem distant and require patience, effort, prayer, and perseverance. What matters is that the true steward does not stop caring for the field simply because the conditions are difficult. He does not allow changing emotions, restless thoughts, tiredness, or difficulties in life to make him careless. He remembers that the atma has been entrusted to him as something sacred, and therefore he continues to tend it with gratitude. One can be a careful custodian or a careless one. The careful custodian continually removes the weeds of ego, distraction, laziness, resentment, and forgetfulness, while nourishing the inner field through devotion, remembrance, prayer, and love. But the steward must also understand that preparing the field does not mean he can command the Divine Farmer to sow. The SatGuru gives according to His wisdom and according to the time He knows is right. His grace cannot be demanded or forced. There are therefore two different sources of happiness. The first is the happiness of loving the responsibility of being a custodian of the Divine spark within, to find joy in caring for what Sri Hari has already given. The second is the happiness that comes when the SatGuru, in His wisdom, bestows a deeper seed of spiritual wisdom and love. Both are complete in themselves, because both are connected to Bhagavan’s Satchitananda. We should not wait for some future spiritual experience before becoming grateful for what has already been given. Life itself is a gift. The opportunity to know Bhagavan, to remember Him, to serve, to love, and to care for the atma is already a great blessing. Therefore, become steady, tend your inner field with love, and do not become discouraged when the harvest does not come immediately. Your task is to prepare the field faithfully. The SatGuru’s task is to know when the seed should be planted. When the field is ready and the Divine Farmer sees that the time has come, He Himself will come and sow what He has been holding within His heart. Until then, find joy in caring for the field you have already been given.
