The One Who Would Not Leave Alone Bandi Chhor Divas*
When Guru Hargobind Sahib Ji was granted release from Gwalior Fort, fifty-two kings were still held there with him, and he refused to leave without them. He was told only those who could hold on to his robe might go, so a robe was made with fifty two tassels. Every king took hold of one and walked out beside him.
He was free to go and he stayed. That choice makes Vishwananda wonder whether we understand liberation at all. We usually think of freedom as the end of our own suffering. We pray to be released from our troubles, attachments and fears, and even on the spiritual path we can get so absorbed in our own liberation that we barely notice the prisons others live in. Staying took nothing from the Guru’s freedom, perhaps because he had never been a prisoner. The walls confined his body and never touched his compassion.
So, we have to ask ourself how often we have walked away from someone because their pain was inconvenient, how often we kept our peace at the cost of someone else’s loneliness, and how often we called indifference detachment. Being free of the world is easy to want. Being free enough to love it without being bound by it is what the SadGuru lived for.
He didn’t ask anyone to deserve a tassel first. He made room for every hand. Grace may be the same, his hand always held out while we stare at our walls and forget to reach for it. If one is holding on to his robe, are we also willing to be a tassel for someone else? We can offer patience where there’s anger, make room for someone who feels shut out, and loosen the fear, judgment and loneliness we tie around each other. One liberation includes the chains one stops placing on others. Tonight we light lamps for the SadGuru’s return. Beside one have to ask whether one have grown a little less likely to imprison someone with our expectations or our need to be right, and a little more able to love without possessing, help without controlling and give another soul room.
The deepest prison may be the one we build around our own hearts and call safety. The greatest freedom may be to live fully in this world, free within, and still be able to say, “I will not forget you when the door opens for me.” That’s what I want this year’s lamp to stand for, so that no one ever feels imprisoned because of me.
