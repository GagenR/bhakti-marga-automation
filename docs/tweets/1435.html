*The Rakhi We Can't See!!!*
Raksha Bandhan comes around again this week and the whole festival rests on a piece of thread. A sister ties it on her brother's wrist, applies tilak and then everyone eats too many sweets. Rakṣā means protection. Bandhan means a bond, something knotted so it holds.This year, August 28, a few events coincide. Balarāma Jayanti when observed on Shravana Purnima is celebrated on Raksha Bandhan. The appearance of Krishna's elder brother, whose name carries bala, strength. Then, there is also a lunar eclipse at the same time this year.
Thread, which takes the center stage is the strange part because it can't protect anybody. It gets wet in the shower, frays and eventually falls off. Long before this festival becoming mostly about siblings, the rakṣā - sūtra was tied on as a prayer for someone's safekeeping. This was a way of saying may dharma guard you and may the divine stay close wherever you're going. The thread was never doing the work. The prayer inside it was.
This is why it opens up differently through bhakti. The rakhi that actually holds isn't the one on the wrist. It's the invisible thread between the devotee and the Guru and Lord. We wander off from that bond constantly and manage to convince ourselves that we are alone. It holds through all of that without asking our permission. Rakṣā seems to mean something other than a guarantee that life will go easy on us. It means that whatever comes, we aren't walking into it by ourselves.
Balarāma arrives on a day already about elder brothers. He walked beside Krishna through Vraja and stood in front of him when standing there was needed. This is exactly what a sister asks for when she ties the thread. His name Bala, carries a kind of strength that has nothing to do with the body. It's the strength to keep practicing on mornings when enthusiasm has dried up, to forgive someone who never apologized, to serve when nobody's counting your seva and to hold on to God when life refuses to follow the plan we made for it. Love without that remains a sentiment. This is the other half of what rakṣā means. A thread ties protection onto us from outside. Bala is the protection that has to grow inside us, the strength to guard our own remembrance of God when nobody else can do it for us.
And the last event of the day is the moon going dark. Our tradition has long read the moon as the mind. Ours goes into shadow with almost no provocation. One conversation does it. One criticism, one person who behaved nothing like we expected and the peace of the last few months is gone. That's when we start asking where God went.
Look at what an eclipse actually is. The moon hasn't gone anywhere. The sun hasn't stopped burning for a second. Something has simply moved between the light and the thing receiving it. Inside us that something is usually our fear or pride or an old wound we won't put down. If the light had vanished there would be nothing left to do. If something is only blocking it, the work is remembrance.This is the protection the thread is really asking for. We pour enormous energy into guarding our bodies, our things, our reputations. All of it will change. Maybe the greatest protection the Lord gives is protection from forgetting him. The Satguru never promises that no shadow will cross our lives. He gives a way through it: mantra, sādhana and sevā leaving the mind turning back towards the heart again. It's the rakhi none of us can see and the only one that doesn't fall off. And eclipses end. Nobody relights the sun.
So tie the thread this week. Celebrate the brothers and sisters God gave us in this life and the people who once stood beside us. Ask Balarāma for the strength to get through whatever keeps standing between us and God. Then, without telling anyone, tie one more rakhi around the heart. Offer it to the Guru and the Lord and ask for the only protection worth asking for. Protect my remembrance of you. When life is beautiful, let me remember you.
