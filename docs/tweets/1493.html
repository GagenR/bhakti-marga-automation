Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

When something starts to disappear, a profound shift happens.
Amavasya is the day of the disappearance of the moon. When the night is the darkest. We live most of our lives caught in the dance of presence attracted to the waxing, the visible, the loud, accumulating, amassing, more, more, and more. Yet, spiritual realization rarely happens when the cup is full; it begins when things start to disappear, because that’s when one really pays attention. Darkness is not merely the absence of light,it is the creative potency from which light is born, the quiet canvas upon which existence rests. The physical disappearance of the moon symbolizes (1) the dissolving of the ego, the intellect, and the individual mind, which is closely tied to lunar energy, and (2) the emergence of the great divine power from within of unconditional love and connection to Bhagavan Sri Hari.
When external light and connections vanish, one really looks inward. Amavasya provides a natural cosmic support to turn away from sensory pleasures and devote oneself to the love of the Antaryami within. In total darkness, eyes become useless, stimulating the inner vision to open. What feels like loss to the mind is recognized by the seeker as limitless space and potential. Hence, bhaktas should never fear darkness, know the Sadguru to be higher than all. The thread of a bhakta’s life is always connected to His lotus feet. When darkness surrounds one’s life, when there is no moon to guide the traveler, the grace of the Sadguru becomes the inner Sun, illuminating everything. That inner illumination is so bright that no darkness can touch one again, that inner vision of His sacred feet takes over any delusion of the mind. When your external cherished attachments, identities, and structures begin to disappear, a real Guru does not stop the dissolution. Instead, He will hold the space, reminding you that what is disappearing was never truly yours, and what remains, the pure, unchanging love of Sri Hari can never be lost.
