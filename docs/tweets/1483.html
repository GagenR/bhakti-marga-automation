Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

woensdag 30 september 2026, 12:07
The SatGuru bestows upon the bhakta the vision of both Saguna Brahman and the realization of Nirguna Brahman the Divine with form and the Divine beyond form. Saguna is Bhagavān as He can be loved, worshipped, contemplated, and experienced; Nirguna is that same Supreme Reality beyond name, form, qualities, and concepts. The SatGuru reveals that these are not two separate truths, but two dimensions of the same Reality. He gives this grace without expectation, opening a possibility of realization that was once considered exceedingly rare. Yet the greatest obstacle is often not the absence of grace, but the inability to recognize it. The mind is easily pulled back into distraction, desire, boredom, laziness, and the endless concerns of ordinary life. One can stand before something sacred and, through familiarity, fail to perceive its magnitude. This is why there is such urgency in the spiritual path, not because the SatGuru demands anything, but because human attention is precious and life continually moves forward. The SatGuru waits with immense love and hope for the moment when the bhakta’s longing becomes simple and wholehearted: not for another temporary experience, achievement, or possession, but for the Eternal itself, for SriHari love and for Truth. Purnatva, or fullness, points to a Reality in which nothing is lacking. The SatGuru is not merely imparting spiritual knowledge; He is opening the possibility of moving beyond concepts into direct realization. This is reflected in Shoonya Avastha. Shoonya means emptiness or zero, but in its deeper sense it is not mere nothingness. When every limitation and identification falls away, what remains is limitless potential, emptiness that contains the possibility of everything. Such a state cannot be fully captured by thought; it must be experienced. Yet even here, the bhakta remains free. The SatGuru does not force realization upon anyone. This is the deeper meaning of Tathastu, “so be it.” Each being is allowed to move through the experiences they choose, whether they turn toward the temporary or toward the Eternal. The SatGuru’s love remains present regardless. Thus, the teaching is not about rejecting the world, but about recognizing what is ultimately real within and beyond it. One may fulfil every worldly responsibility while inwardly turning toward the Divine. The essential transformation occurs when the heart stops asking, “What can I receive?” and begins to ask, “What is True? What is Eternal? How can I know That?” The gate is open, the grace is freely given, and the SatGuru waits without coercion. Each being will eventually turn toward that Truth, whether in this life or after countless lifetimes. But when such an opportunity is already before us, the deepest wisdom is simply to recognize it and not allow the mind to overlook what the heart has been seeking all along.
