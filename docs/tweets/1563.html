Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

dinsdag 10 november 2026, 22:58
The moment we begin to believe that our position, knowledge, achievements or spiritual accomplishments make us independent, we begin to move away from humility. Even devotion can become a source of pride when we start believing that we are more deserving, more advanced or more important than others. True spirituality does not make us bigger in our own eyes; it makes our ego smaller before the Divine. When Indra finally recognised his mistake, he approached Krishna in humility. His pride gave way to surrender, and the one who had unleashed the storm eventually bowed before the very Lord he had failed to recognise. Along with Surabhi, the divine cow, he worshipped Krishna, who was then glorified as Govinda, the protector and beloved of the cows and the giver of joy to the senses and the earth. What a beautiful transformation! The storm of arrogance ended in the worship of the Lord. This teaches us that even our mistakes can become turning points when we have the courage to acknowledge them and return to God with a sincere heart. Krishna did not destroy Indra; He removed the illusion that had separated Indra from humility. Perhaps this is one of the greatest expressions of divine compassion: the Lord does not merely protect us from the consequences of the world; He also protects us from the blindness of our own ego. Beneath Govardhan, everyone stood together. No one claimed a separate shelter, no one demanded a greater share of protection and no one was excluded. This is the beauty of divine grace and the true spirit of Annakūṭa: what comes from the Lord is offered back to Him with gratitude and shared with all. The mountain of food reminds us that abundance is sacred when it becomes an offering and that devotion becomes complete when it expresses itself through compassion, generosity and service. So, when life brings its storms, do not lose heart because the rain has not stopped. Do not mistake silence for absence or difficulty for abandonment. Hold on to the Divine, remain close to your SadGuru and allow faith to become the ground upon which you stand. We may not always know how long the storm will last, but we can always remember who is holding the mountain. The greatest blessing is not that we never encounter storms, but that we discover the One who never leaves us beneath them. Vishwananda said, ‘ Let the rain fall, let the winds blow, let the world change around you.’ Stay beneath the mountain of His grace. For when Sri Krishna is your shelter, even the fiercest storm cannot take away the peace of a heart that has surrendered to Him.
