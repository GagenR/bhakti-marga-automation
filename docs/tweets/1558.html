*Gaṇeśa, Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī Visits*
On Dīpāvalī night three deities sit together on the altar. Gaṇeśa sits with Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī. In many homes the new account books are placed before them and śubh lābh, auspicious gain, is written on the door. Together the three become the foundation for the year ahead. We pray to Gaṇeśa first, as we always do. He removes obstacles and the deepest obstacle in us is a lack of discernment to know what is good. Lakṣmī may bring wealth and Sarasvatī may bring learning. Without Gaṇeśa’s wisdom, wealth turns to greed and learning turns to pride. That’s why he comes first. He makes sure the blessings that follow don’t harm us. A tender story is told in our homes. Dīpāvalī falls in the months when Nārāyaṇa rests in divine slumber. Lakṣmī didn’t wish to go from home to home alone on such a night. Pārvatī sent her beloved Gaṇeśa to go with her. Mā comes and brings her child and wherever abundance goes, wisdom walks beside it. Then we pray to Lakṣmī. She is Śrī, far more than wealth. She is beauty, nourishment, harmony and the grace that makes a house feel like a home. A family can have plenty and still feel poor, and a simple home can feel rich. Lakṣmī is the difference. She lives where there is gratitude and leaves where there is quarrel. Then we pray to Sarasvatī, the mother of knowledge, music and speech. Her blessing is clarity. She helps us learn, speak truthfully and understand what we read in the scriptures. Her place on this night tells us that true prosperity includes a mind that keeps growing. The three of them are intertwined. Wealth without knowledge is blind because it doesn’t know how to serve. Knowledge without wealth often can’t act because it has no means to help. Both without discernment fall over. With all three together, one can hold firm.
The three also show us what to do with each blessing. Gaṇeśa’s wisdom turns gain into śubh lābh, gain that is auspicious. Lakṣmī’s wealth becomes dāna and seva, charity and service. Sarasvatī’s knowledge becomes teaching and devotion. In each case the gift flows back toward God. So, as you bow before the three, pray simply. Gaṇeśa, give me the wisdom to use what I receive. Mā Lakṣmī, let your grace live in this home. Mā Sarasvatī, keep my mind clear and my words true. Then let the year begin on this foundation.
