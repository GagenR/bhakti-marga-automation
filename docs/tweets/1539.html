Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

vrijdag 30 oktober 2026, 16:40
Accept the present as it is, remain content and happy, and allow the medicine of the Guru’s love to transform you from within. The love of the Guru is a divine remedy that gradually heals the wounds of the heart, quietens the restless mind, and teaches the soul to discover peace in the midst of life’s uncertainties. We spend so much of our lives longing for a different tomorrow, regretting yesterday, and struggling against the circumstances of today that we forget to experience the very life that has been given to us. We imagine that happiness will come when we acquire more wealth, achieve greater success, fulfil our desires, or finally reach a place where nothing troubles us. Yet, the mind that has not learned contentment will continue to seek more, even after receiving everything it once desired. The greatest rest in life is to accept the present moment with gratitude and remain peaceful within, regardless of what circumstances may bring. Everyone is searching for rest, peace, relaxation, and that indescribable feeling of bliss which makes the heart feel complete. Yet so few understand that lasting peace cannot be found merely by changing the world around us; it begins when we allow the heart to change within. We exhaust ourselves pursuing things that promise happiness, only to discover that the mind remains restless even after obtaining them. Remember this truth: no matter how much you accumulate throughout your lifetime, if you never learn to enjoy what you have been given, your accumulation will become a burden rather than a blessing. You may spend your entire life gathering wealth, securing possessions, building a future, and worrying about what tomorrow might bring, while the simple joys of today quietly pass you by. You may postpone your happiness until the next achievement, the next opportunity, or the next season of life, believing that one day you will finally be free to rest. But life does not wait for us to finish all our worries. The years pass, the body grows tired, and the moments that could have been filled with gratitude, love, prayer, and peaceful living become memories that can never be recovered. In the end, you may leave behind everything you spent your life accumulating, and those who come after you may enjoy what you were too anxious, fearful, or preoccupied to enjoy yourself. This is one of the quiet ironies of worldly existence. Many families spend generations accumulating wealth, yet the very people who gather it live with the fear of losing it. They possess enough to live comfortably, but their minds remain occupied with protecting, increasing, and preserving what they have. They use only a small portion of their resources, deny themselves simple comforts, and postpone their happiness in the name of security. Eventually, what they carefully accumulated passes into the hands of the next generation, while their own precious years have already slipped away. There is nothing wrong with working hard, providing for our loved ones, saving for the future, or being responsible with what we receive. These things, too, can be acts of love and wisdom. But we must never allow the pursuit of security to steal the peace of the present, nor allow the fear of tomorrow to prevent us from living gratefully today. Wealth is useful when it serves life; it becomes a burden when life is sacrificed merely to preserve wealth. Possessions are blessings when they bring comfort and enable us to serve others; they become chains when our hearts become attached to them. The true question is not how much we have gathered, but whether we have learned to live with gratitude, generosity, and inner freedom. Enjoy the present. This is the secret. Enjoy the food placed before you, the people who love you, the opportunity to serve, the quietness of prayer, and the little moments of grace that often go unnoticed. Do not wait for every problem to disappear before allowing yourself to smile.
