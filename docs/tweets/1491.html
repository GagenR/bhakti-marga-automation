Paramahamsa Sri Swami Vishwananda

woensdag 7 oktober 2026, 17:16
Soon Pitru Paksha will come to a close, and Navaratri will begin. The connection between these sacred periods is far deeper than a change from one festival to another. For fifteen days, under the waning moon, the movement of consciousness turns inward. The outer world gradually becomes quieter, and we are invited to remember the ancient lineage of Rishis and Sages who carried Dharma through the ages, and the ancestors whose blood, sacrifices, struggles, prayers, and blessings brought us to the very place where we stand today. In Sanatan Dharma, we do not see ourselves as isolated individuals. We are links in an immense spiritual chain. Every person is born within a sacred Gotra, a lineage connected to a particular Rishi. For devotees initiated under the protection of the Hari Bhakta Sampradaya, the Gotra of the SatGuru is passed on to them. Spiritually, this establishes an alignment with the current of the SatGuru’s lineage, a sacred stream ultimately directed toward Sri Hari Narayan and Mahalakshmi. This is why Tarpana is so significant. Through water, food, prayers, charity, remembrance, and gratitude, we acknowledge those who came before us and recognize that much of what sustains our lives was placed into our hands by those whom we may never have known. Pitru Paksha is therefore not simply about remembering the dead. It is about understanding inheritance. We inherit not only a body and a name, but tendencies, impressions, responsibilities, blessings, unfinished obligations, and karmic patterns. Pitru Rina reminds us that life itself carries a sacred debt of gratitude. We cannot repay our ancestors merely through material means; we honor them by living consciously, purifying what has been passed down, and refusing to carry unconscious patterns further into the future. As Pitru Paksha reaches its culmination in Mahalaya Amavasya, the outer light of the waning lunar cycle reaches its deepest darkness. Yet spiritually, darkness here is not emptiness. It is the hidden womb. It is the mysterious ground in which seeds are buried before they emerge into manifestation. What is surrendered, purified, prayed over, and consciously planted in this sacred darkness becomes the seed of what may emerge with the coming light. And then comes Navaratri. From the silence of the ancestors, we turn toward Shakti the Divine Mother, the supreme cosmic power of creation, preservation, protection, destruction, and transformation. The movement is profound: first we remember where we came from; then we purify what we have inherited; then we enter the darkness of Mahalaya; and from that darkness, Shakti arises. Therefore, as these final days of Pitru Paksha remain, let the prayers become deeper, the offerings more sincere, the charity more selfless, and the gratitude more conscious. Do not simply ask for blessings. Offer peace. Offer food. Offer water. Offer remembrance. Offer your actions. Let whatever is unresolved within the ancestral stream be surrendered at the feet of the Divine. Mahalaya Amavasya is the cosmic midnight , the fertile silence before manifestation. What we sow there matters. Then, as Navaratri dawns, may the Divine Mother awaken within us and transform those seeds into Dharma, strength, devotion, wisdom, and spiritual liberation. First we bow to the past. Then we enter the silence. Then Shakti awakens. And from that awakening, a new cycle begins.
